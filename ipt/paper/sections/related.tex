\section{Related Work}
\label{sec:related}

\paragraph{Predicting responses to unseen perturbations.}
Latent-shift models represent a perturbation as a vector added in a learned latent space, as in scGen~\cite{lotfollahi2019scgen} and the compositional perturbation autoencoder CPA~\cite{lotfollahi2023predicting}; biolord disentangles known cell attributes from the remaining variation~\cite{piran2024biolord}.
These models generalise to an unseen gene only through a description of that gene, such as a gene-ontology embedding.
GEARS learns gene and perturbation embeddings over a co-expression graph and a Gene Ontology graph and was designed for unseen single and combinatorial perturbations~\cite{roohani2024predicting}.
Foundation and language models pretrained on large cell atlases, scGPT~\cite{cui2024scgpt}, Cell2Sentence~\cite{levine2024cell2sentence} and STATE~\cite{adduri2025predicting}, transfer learned cell-state representations to perturbation prediction, and SPECTRA predicts responses with a directed heterophilic graph neural network over an inferred GRN~\cite{calabro2026spectra}.
iPerturb differs from all of them in fixing the form of regulatory propagation (a Hill transfer under a clamp) and in separating regulatory from associative edges.

\paragraph{Evaluating perturbation-response models.}
Benchmarks show that deep perturbation models often fail to surpass a linear model on a low-rank gene embedding or the mean training response~\cite{ahlmanneltze2025comparison}, and that a response shared by most perturbations inflates correlation-based metrics~\cite{vinas2025systema}.
We therefore centre all responses on the training-mean response, report a magnitude-free correlation, compare against the linear low-rank baseline, and test every network against degree-preserving rewired and data-derived graphs with the same read-out.

\paragraph{Regulatory networks and their context.}
Curated resources collect signed TF--target interactions from the literature, including TRRUST~\cite{han2018trrust}, OmniPath~\cite{turei2016omnipath} and CollecTRI~\cite{mullerdott2023collectri}; GeneHancer links enhancers to target genes with tissue annotations~\cite{fishilevich2017genehancer}; STRING~\cite{szklarczyk2023string} and COXPRESdb~\cite{obayashi2023coxpresdb} provide protein association and co-expression.
Context-specific GRNs are usually inferred from single-cell multi-omics, as in SCENIC+~\cite{bravogonzalezbl2023scenicplus}, and CellOracle uses such inferred GRNs to simulate TF knockouts by repeated matrix multiplication~\cite{kamimoto2023dissecting}.
iPerturb instead combines public evidence tiers with the training perturbations of the same cell line, keeps association evidence undirected and unsigned, and simulates the perturbation with a persistent clamp whose steady state is proven to exist and to be unique.

\paragraph{Mechanistic and biologically informed models.}
Hill-type and thermodynamic models describe how the occupancy of a promoter by activators and repressors sets transcription~\cite{bintu2005transcriptional,alon2007introduction}.
Continuous logic models turn such transfer functions into ordinary differential equations on signalling networks~\cite{wittmann2009}, and cell-line-specific versions with a normalised Hill transfer have been fitted with one set of parameters per edge~\cite{eduati2017}; PHOENIX fits neural ODEs of gene expression with physiological priors~\cite{alhassen2024phoenix}.
Biologically informed neural networks shape their architecture by prior knowledge~\cite{selby2025beyond}: Genetic Neural Networks follow TF regulation with one layer per gene~\cite{eetemadi2019genetic}, and knowledge-primed and pathway-informed networks predict cell states or clinical phenotypes~\cite{fortelny2020knowledge,elmarakeby2021biologically,lotfollahi2023biologically}.
iPerturb shares the Hill parameter of a regulator across its targets, keeps feedback, and predicts genome-wide expression changes rather than a phenotype.

\paragraph{Network propagation.}
Propagating a signal over a biological network, by random walks with restart, diffusion kernels or truncated walks, amplifies weak signals by integrating all paths between genes, and the normalisation of the walk controls the bias towards hubs~\cite{cowen2017network}.
iPerturb uses propagation twice: signed, clamped propagation of the perturbation over regulatory edges, and multi-hop propagation of observed training responses over association edges, integrated across evidence tiers.
Learnable univariate functions in a basis expansion, whose evaluation reduces to matrix multiplication, underlie learnable-activation networks~\cite{liu2024kan,li2024fastkan}; we use a Chebyshev expansion to keep Hill transfer functions cheap, including edge-specific shapes.
