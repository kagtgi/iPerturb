\section{Data and Methods}
\label{sec:methods}

\subsection{Datasets}
\label{sec:data}
Public gene-regulation resources were curated from four complementary source types: (i)~transcription factor (TF)--target interactions from TRRUST~\cite{han2018trrust}, CollecTRI~\cite{mullerdott2023collectri} and OmniPath~\cite{turei2016omnipath} (\datanum{l1_unique} unique directed pairs, \datanum{l1_signed} of them with a defensible sign); (ii)~enhancer--gene and TF-binding annotations from GeneHancer v5.26~\cite{fishilevich2017genehancer}; (iii)~protein--protein association pairs from STRING~\cite{szklarczyk2023string} filtered at combined score $\ge 700$; and (iv)~co-expression pairs from COXPRESdb~\cite{obayashi2023coxpresdb}.
Perturbation data are the genome-scale CRISPRi Perturb-seq screens of Replogle et al.~\cite{replogle2022mapping} in K562 (chronic myelogenous leukaemia) and RPE1 (retinal pigment epithelium) cells. Starting from the raw UMI counts, each cell was normalised to $10^4$ counts and log1p-transformed; the response of a perturbation $p$ is the \emph{mean-level} difference $\Delta_p=\overline{x}_p-\overline{x}_{0}$ between the mean log-normalised expression of its cells and of the control cells of the same gem groups (batch-matched), computed on the stored gene axis (\datanum{k562_genes} genes in K562, \datanum{rpe1_genes} in RPE1).
Perturbations with fewer than 20 cells and targets absent from the gene axis were removed, leaving \datanum{k562_targets} (K562) and \datanum{rpe1_targets} (RPE1) targets. The data are single-cell, but the predicted endpoint is the perturbation-conditioned mean response; cell-resolved generation is outside the scope of this work.

\subsection{Preprocessing}
\label{sec:prep}
Perturb-seq data are processed in three representations rather than one matrix: control cells for the baseline state, raw UMI counts for count-level effects, and perturbation effects with uncertainty. Cell quality control is applied per gem group with 5-median-absolute-deviation rules on log UMI and log detected genes (both sides) and on the mitochondrial fraction (upper side only; no universal mitochondrial cutoff, because knockdowns can change metabolism); cells carrying more than one guide target are excluded, and control cells are matched to perturbed cells by gem group. We apply no batch correction, no imputation and no cell-cycle regression, since each can remove or create perturbation-specific signal. QC kept \datanum{prep_k562_kept_pct}\% of the \datanum{prep_k562_cells} K562 cells and \datanum{prep_rpe1_kept_pct}\% of the \datanum{prep_rpe1_cells} RPE1 cells (\datanum{prep_k562_fail_genes} and \datanum{prep_rpe1_fail_genes} cells failed the detected-gene rule; controls: \datanum{prep_k562_ctrl_cells} cells in \datanum{prep_k562_ctrl_batches} gem groups for K562, \datanum{prep_rpe1_ctrl_cells} in \datanum{prep_rpe1_ctrl_batches} for RPE1).
The effect of a perturbation is the difference of mean log-normalised expression to the batch-matched controls ($\Delta$, Section~\ref{sec:data}) with a Welch standard error, a pseudobulk $\log_2$ fold change computed from summed UMIs (library-size normalised, pseudocount), and the knockdown efficiency of the target, $\hat k_p=1-\bar x_{p,\mathrm{target}}/\bar x_{\mathrm{control,target}}$ on the linear scale, reported only when the target is detected in controls ($\ge5$ CPM); otherwise it is left unknown rather than set to complete knockdown. Cells and guides are not treated as independent replicates: the standard error is descriptive and is used for response labels and precision weights only. Gene sets, graph edges, loadings and all hyper-parameters are fitted on training perturbations only; the 8k-gene axis is the full stored gene axis of the screen (targets are kept even when they are not highly variable).

\subsection{Constructing the context-specific GRN}
\label{sec:grn}
\begin{algorithm}[!tb]
\caption{iPerturb: context-specific GRN assembly and integrated propagation}
\label{alg:iperturb}
\small
\begin{algorithmic}[1]
\Require evidence tiers $\mathcal{E}_1,\dots,\mathcal{E}_4$; cell line $c$; training and validation perturbations with responses $(\mathcal{P}_{\mathrm{tr}},\Delta_{\mathrm{tr}})$, $(\mathcal{P}_{\mathrm{va}},\Delta_{\mathrm{va}})$; kernel grid $\mathcal{K}$; held-out target $t$
\Ensure predicted response $\hat\Delta_t$; signed paths and supporting neighbours of $t$
\State $A_\ell \gets$ edges of $\mathcal{E}_\ell$ on the gene axis of $c$, Level~2 active in $c$ \Comment{\emph{Phase I}}
\State resolve duplicates by the lowest tier; set conflicting signs to unsigned
\State $R \gets$ signed, directed edges of Level~1 \Comment{regulatory core}
\State $\mathcal{A} \gets \{A_\ell\}$ as undirected, unsigned graphs \Comment{association tiers}
\State clamp $x_t \gets x_t^{0}(1-\zeta)$ and iterate Eq.~\eqref{eq:fp} on $R$ \Comment{\emph{Phase II}}
\State report the signed paths from $t$ and their supporting databases \Comment{Eq.~\eqref{eq:mech}}
\State $\hat\Delta^{(0)} \gets$ linear low-rank prediction fitted on $\mathcal{P}_{\mathrm{tr}}$
\For{each tier graph $A_\ell \in \mathcal{A}$}
  \For{each kernel $\kappa \in \mathcal{K}$ and degree correction $\gamma$}
    \State $S \gets \kappa(A_\ell)$;\quad $\hat\Delta^{(\ell)}_p \gets \sum_{j\in\mathcal{P}_{\mathrm{tr}}\setminus\{p\}} S_{pj}\deg_j^{-\gamma}\Delta_j \,/\, \sum_{j} S_{pj}\deg_j^{-\gamma}$
  \EndFor
  \State keep the $(\kappa,\gamma)$ with lowest validation error; $\hat\Delta^{(\ell)}_p\gets\hat\Delta^{(0)}_p$ if $p$ reaches no training target
\EndFor
\State $w \gets \mathrm{NNLS}\big([\hat\Delta^{(0)},\hat\Delta^{(1)},\dots,\hat\Delta^{(L)}]_{\mathcal{P}_{\mathrm{va}}},\Delta_{\mathrm{va}}\big)$;\quad $w \gets w/\textstyle\sum_\ell w_\ell$
\State \Return $\hat\Delta_t=\sum_\ell w_\ell\,\hat\Delta^{(\ell)}_t$ and the top-weighted neighbours of $t$
\end{algorithmic}
\end{algorithm}
To construct the GRN of a cell line, we integrated the evidence of Section~\ref{sec:data} into a five-tier hierarchy ordered by evidence strength.
Level~1 forms the signed, directed backbone: curated TF--target interactions from TRRUST, OmniPath and CollecTRI, extended with transcriptional edges from SIGNOR~\cite{losurdo2023signor}; an edge keeps a sign only when its sources agree.
Level~2 adds unsigned TF--target edges whose regulatory element is active in the cell line: GeneHancer enhancers with a tissue annotation of the line and a TF-binding site of the TF, and, as an extension, the 20 strongest factors per target gene in ChIP-Atlas experiments of the same line (K562 or hTERT RPE-1, $\pm$5\,kb of the transcription start site)~\cite{zou2024chipatlas}.
Level~3 adds protein association, STRING pairs with combined score $\ge 700$, CORUM complex co-membership~\cite{tsitsiridis2023corum} and BioGRID physical interactions~\cite{oughtred2021biogrid}, and Level~4 adds co-expression: at most 20 of the 50 top-ranked COXPRESdb partners of each gene, and the 50 strongest partners per gene in GTEx samples re-aligned by ARCHS4~\cite{gtex2020atlas,lachmann2018archs4}.
Level~5 is the observed response of the training perturbations of the line; it enters the GRN as the signal that is propagated (Section~\ref{sec:model}) and is recomputed for every fold, so no held-out response reaches the network.
Levels~3--4 are association evidence: they are stored as undirected, unsigned edges and never drawn as regulatory arrows.

Cross-tier duplicates were resolved in favour of the lowest-numbered tier, and within-tier sign conflicts defaulted to unsigned, so an unknown sign is never promoted to activation.
Context therefore enters at three points: the gene axis and Level~2 depend on the cell line, Level~5 comes from that line's training perturbations, and the weights that combine the tiers are fitted on that line's validation perturbations.
All tiers are restricted to the gene axis of the screen, and each tier is kept as a separate graph $A_\ell$ so that its contribution can be measured and weighted.
Algorithm~\ref{alg:iperturb} summarises how the tiers are assembled and propagated; the propagation operators are defined in Section~\ref{sec:model}.


\subsection{Learnable propagation that follows the Hill function}
\label{sec:model}
\paragraph{Hill-form regulatory propagation.}
Let $x_i\in(\varepsilon,1-\varepsilon)$ be the regulatory activity of gene $i$ and $x^0_i=\sigma(z_i)$ its baseline, where $z_i$ is the standardised control expression (a fixed monotone map, so the graph cannot be bypassed by a learned encoder). A regulator $j$ passes its activity through a modified Hill transfer function
\begin{equation}
H(x;k,n)=\frac{k^{n}\,[\,1-(1-x)^{n}\,]}{(1-x)^{n}+k^{n}},\qquad H(0)=0,\;H(1)=1,
\label{eq:hill}
\end{equation}
(the TF4 form of the logic-ODE literature~\cite{wittmann2009,eduati2017}) with $n=2$ and one learnable $k_j\in[0.05,1]$ \emph{per regulator}, shared by all of its outgoing edges. Signed messages are baseline-centred, so an unperturbed cell produces exactly zero response:
\begin{equation}
x_i^{t+1}=(1-\eta)\,x_i^{t}+\eta\,\sigma\!\Big(\mathrm{logit}\,x_i^{0}+\sum_{j\to i} w_{ji}\big[H(x_j^{t};k_j)-H(x_j^{0};k_j)\big]\Big),
\label{eq:fp}
\end{equation}
with $w_{ji}=\rho\,s_{ji}\,c_{ji}\,\mathrm{softplus}(a)/\max\{1,\sum_{j'}|\cdot|\}$, where $s_{ji}\in\{-1,+1\}$ is the curated sign, $c_{ji}$ the evidence confidence, and $\rho,a$ are learnable scalars. A CRISPRi knockdown of gene $p$ is a \emph{persistent clamp}, $x_p^{t}=(1-\zeta)x_p^{0}$ at every iteration, where $\zeta$ is the median knockdown of the training perturbations. The fixed point $x^\ast$ is found by damped iteration ($\eta=0.5$, 24 steps) and the predicted response is $\hat\Delta_g=d_g\,(x^\ast_g-x^0_g)$ with a non-negative diagonal decoder $d_g$; no dense $G\times G$ decoder is used, so every prediction decomposes over named edges.

\paragraph{Matrix-multiplication form.}
Per-edge Hill evaluation requires gathering $x_j$ for every edge and scattering messages, which maps poorly to accelerators. We therefore approximate each regulator's Hill curve by a Chebyshev expansion of degree $r$,
\begin{equation}
H(x;k_j)\approx\sum_{b=0}^{r}a_b(k_j)\,T_b(2x-1),
\label{eq:cheb}
\end{equation}
whose coefficients $a_b(k)$ are least-squares fitted to Eq.~\eqref{eq:hill} on a logarithmic grid of $k$ and linearly interpolated in $\log k$, so that $k_j$ stays learnable and differentiable. With $\mathbf h=\hat H(x)-\hat H(x^0)\in\mathbb{R}^{B\times G}$ for a batch of $B$ perturbations, the aggregation in Eq.~\eqref{eq:fp} is one sparse (or dense) matrix product $\mathbf h\,\mathbf W$, with $\mathbf W_{ji}=w_{ji}$, instead of an edge-wise gather and index-add. The same fixed-point equations are solved; only the arithmetic changes (equivalence is verified in Section~\ref{sec:results_hill}).

\paragraph{Clamped steady state and multi-hop explanation.}
For explanations the same network is read as a signed linear system around the baseline. With the perturbed gene $g$ clamped and all other genes $R$ free,
\begin{equation}
\Delta x_R=(I-W_{RR})^{-1}W_{Rg}\,\Delta x_g=\sum_{k\ge1}W_{RR}^{\,k-1}W_{Rg}\,\Delta x_g,
\label{eq:mech}
\end{equation}
where $W_{ij}$ is the signed, confidence-weighted effect of gene $j$ on gene $i$ (activation $>0$, repression $<0$, unknown signs excluded) and $\Delta x_g$ is the log-expression change implied by the partial knockdown (training-median efficiency, not a complete knockout). The $k$-th term is the contribution of regulatory walks of length $k$, so every predicted change decomposes into hops and into signed paths; the strongest paths to a gene are reported together with the databases that support each edge and with the reinforcing and opposing path mass, so that cancellation is visible. We scale $W$ so that $\rho(|W|)=0.9$; Theorem~\ref{thm:neumann} in Appendix~\ref{app:convergence} shows that this makes the multi-hop series converge absolutely for every clamped gene (and Theorem~\ref{thm:contract} gives a unique convergence point for the nonlinear Hill-form iteration, whose gain is parametrised so that it is a contraction for every parameter value). Paths are candidate mechanisms, not proven causal routes.

\paragraph{Response propagation over the association network.}
The association channel generalises the neighbour mean to multi-hop propagation of \emph{training responses} (network propagation~\cite{cowen2017network}). Seeds are training targets carrying their observed response vectors, and a test gene $p$ receives $\hat\Delta_p=\sum_jS_{pj}\Delta_j/\sum_jS_{pj}$ over training targets $j$, where $S$ is a propagation kernel of the association network: a random walk with restart, $S=\alpha(I-(1-\alpha)W)^{-1}$ with $W=AD^{-1}$ or $D^{-1/2}AD^{-1/2}$, a heat kernel $e^{-tL}$, or a walk truncated after $K$ hops, with an optional degree-bias correction (division by $\deg_j^{\gamma}$) and a blend weight with the linear baseline selected on validation perturbations; the kernels converge for every $\alpha\in(0,1]$ and give convex combinations of training responses (Proposition~\ref{prop:kernel}). Networks of different tiers can be propagated separately and combined with non-negative weights fitted on validation perturbations.

\paragraph{Association channel.}
Most perturbed genes are not curated TFs. Let $\mathbf G\in\mathbb{R}^{G\times k}$ be the top-$k$ gene loadings of the centred training responses, and $\hat A$ the row-normalised adjacency of the (undirected) context-specific GRN. The embedding of a perturbed gene $t$ is smoothed over its neighbours, $e_t=(1-\alpha)\mathbf G_t+\alpha\,(\hat A\mathbf G)_t$, and the response is $\hat\Delta_t=\bar\Delta+e_t\,\mathbf W_e\,\mathbf G^\top$ with a ridge-fitted $\mathbf W_e$; $k$ and $\alpha$ are chosen on the validation perturbations from fixed grids. For $\alpha=0$ this is the standard linear low-rank baseline, so the channel nests it.

\paragraph{SCC-aware execution.}
Feedback makes Eq.~\eqref{eq:fp} cyclic. For a frozen graph we compute strongly connected components once, order their condensation topologically, restrict the computation to the nodes reachable from the perturbed gene, settle acyclic singletons with a single forward update and iterate feedback components locally with their upstream inputs fixed. The predictions are those of the global solver (Section~\ref{sec:results_hill}); this is an execution strategy, not a different model.

\subsection{Evaluating iPerturb}
\label{sec:eval}
For each cell line, targets were split into training, validation and test perturbations (70/15/15\%) in three folds stratified only by properties known without response data (TF status, control-expression tertile, curated out-degree bin); no target is shared across the splits of a fold. All graph-derived quantities, loadings, knockdown efficacy and hyper-parameters use training (and validation) perturbations only.
Because the mean response of a perturbation is dominated by a response shared across perturbations~\cite{vinas2025systema}, the primary metric is the Pearson correlation of $\Delta$ \emph{centred} on the training-mean response ($\rho_c$), computed per perturbation without the perturbed gene and averaged over perturbations; we also report the same correlation after projecting the training-mean direction out of both profiles ($\rho_r$, which removes response magnitude), the top-20 centred correlation ($\rho_{20}$), the mean normalised rank of the correct perturbation among all test perturbations (Cent.; 0.5 is chance) and the area under the precision--recall curve for differentially expressed genes (|Welch $z|>4$, cell level, no biological replicates, prevalence-dominated by the shared response). Intervals are 95\% bootstrap intervals over unique held-out targets (each target averaged over the folds in which it is tested); paired differences use the same bootstrap.
We compare against no change, the training-mean response, a ridge model on a low-rank gene embedding~\cite{ahlmanneltze2025comparison}, GEARS~\cite{roohani2024predicting}, CPA~\cite{lotfollahi2023predicting} and biolord~\cite{piran2024biolord} trained on the same folds, released checkpoints of scGPT~\cite{cui2024scgpt} and STATE~\cite{adduri2025predicting} (Appendix~\ref{app:external}), a two-layer graph convolutional network on the same GRN, and control GRNs with degree-preserving rewired edges or a graph learned from the training data alone. The full protocol was fixed in a hashed pre-registration file before fitting, and later analyses are labelled as such in the repository.
