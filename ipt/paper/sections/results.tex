\section{Results}
\label{sec:results}

\subsection{iPerturb assembles cell-line-specific tiers around a small signed core}
\label{sec:results_grn}
Context enters the network through the GeneHancer tier (Level~2) and, when used, through the training-perturbation tier (Level~5); the curated tier (Level~1) and the STRING tier (Level~3) are the same resources for every cell line and differ between lines only through the gene axis. Restricted to enhancers active in each line and to TFs whose binding sites match, the GeneHancer tier contains \datanum{g7c_n_edges_K562} TF--target pairs for K562, \datanum{g7c_n_edges_HepG2} for HepG2 and \datanum{g7c_n_edges_Jurkat} for Jurkat, yet the edge sets overlap little (Jaccard index \datanum{g7c_jaccard_HepG2_K562} K562--HepG2, \datanum{g7c_jaccard_Jurkat_K562} K562--Jurkat, \datanum{g7c_jaccard_HepG2_Jurkat} HepG2--Jurkat), and TF out-degrees agree only moderately across lines (Spearman $\rho=$ \datanum{g7c_tf_outdeg_spearman_HepG2_K562} K562--HepG2 to \datanum{g7c_tf_outdeg_spearman_Jurkat_K562} K562--Jurkat). The RPE1 tier is empty in this release because no RPE1-labelled TF-binding annotation is available to match, so RPE1 relies on the other tiers.
The curated signed core is small relative to the screens. Only \datanum{k562_signed_out_pct}\% (K562) and \datanum{rpe1_signed_out_pct}\% (RPE1) of the perturbed genes have a signed out-edge, and \datanum{k562_reach100_pct}\% and \datanum{rpe1_reach100_pct}\% reach at least 100 genes. Where signed edges exist they are informative: on training perturbations, the sign of a curated edge predicts the direction of the knockdown response of its target in \datanum{k562_concord}\% (K562; \datanum{k562_resp_edges} responsive edges) and \datanum{rpe1_concord}\% (RPE1; \datanum{rpe1_resp_edges}) of cases (binomial $p<$ \datanum{binom_p_max}; edges that share a TF are not independent, and a per-TF test over the \datanum{k562_n_tf} and \datanum{rpe1_n_tf} TFs with at least five responsive edges gives $p=$ \datanum{k562_tf_wilcoxon_p} and \datanum{rpe1_tf_wilcoxon_p}). The core is therefore reliable but narrow, which motivates the association channel of Section~\ref{sec:model}.

\paragraph{Preprocessing.} The careful pipeline of Section~\ref{sec:prep} changes the targets very little: responses recomputed after per-gem-group QC correlate with the earlier ones at a median of \datanum{prep_k562_agree} (K562; 5th percentile \datanum{prep_k562_agree_p05}) and \datanum{prep_rpe1_agree} (RPE1; \datanum{prep_rpe1_agree_p05}), as expected for screens that were already filtered. Knockdown is partial and heterogeneous: the knockdown of the target was detectable for \datanum{prep_k562_kd_det} K562 and \datanum{prep_rpe1_kd_det} RPE1 targets, with a median efficiency of \datanum{prep_k562_kd_med}\% and \datanum{prep_rpe1_kd_med}\%, and \datanum{prep_k562_kd_lt50}\% and \datanum{prep_rpe1_kd_lt50}\% of targets below 50\%, yet efficiency is essentially uncorrelated with the size of the response (Spearman $\rho=$ \datanum{prep_k562_kd_spearman} and \datanum{prep_rpe1_kd_spearman}). Weighting training perturbations and the other design choices that did not matter are examined in Section~\ref{sec:ablation}.

\subsection{The tiered GRN improves prediction of held-out perturbations}
\label{sec:results_pred}
\begin{figure}[t]
\centering
\includegraphics[width=\linewidth]{figures/fig3_generalization.pdf}
\caption{\textbf{The tiered GRN improves response prediction for unseen CRISPRi targets in two human cell lines.}
Mean over held-out targets with 95\% bootstrap intervals over unique targets (three target-held-out folds; K562 $n=$ \datanum{k562_nuniq}, RPE1 $n=$ \datanum{rpe1_nuniq}). Filled circles, K562; open squares, RPE1.
\textbf{(a)}~Pearson correlation of the response after removing the training-mean response ($\rho_c$, primary; the train mean scores 0 by construction).
\textbf{(b)}~The same after projecting the training-mean direction out of both profiles ($\rho_r$).
\textbf{(c)}~Top-20 centred correlation.
\textbf{(d)}~Mean normalised rank of the correct perturbation (centroid accuracy; 0.5 is chance).
Smoothing with a rewired GRN or a data-kNN graph are controls with the same capacity and tuning grid.}
\label{fig:results}
\end{figure}
Because the mean response is dominated by a component shared across perturbations, raw correlation is saturated by the training mean (\datanum{k562_b1_raw} in K562 and \datanum{rpe1_b1_raw} in RPE1), and we therefore report centred correlations (Figure~\ref{fig:results}, Table~\ref{tab:main}).
The smoothing head of iPerturb, in which a perturbed gene's embedding is averaged over its neighbours in the GRN built from the curated and protein-association tiers (L1+L3), improves on the linear low-rank baseline by \datanum{k562_d_ip_b2_rc} (K562) and \datanum{rpe1_d_ip_b2_rc} (RPE1) in $\rho_c$, and by \datanum{k562_d_ip_b2_rr} and \datanum{rpe1_d_ip_b2_rr} after removing the shared response direction ($\rho_r$), so the gain is not a change in response magnitude. The same model on a degree-preserving rewired GRN gains nothing (the real GRN is better by \datanum{k562_d_ip_rew_rc} and \datanum{rpe1_d_ip_rew_rc}), and neither does a graph learned from the training data alone with the same degree sequence (the GRN is better by \datanum{k562_d_ip_knn_rc} and \datanum{rpe1_d_ip_knn_rc}). Zeroing each training row's own-target entry before the decomposition leaves the gain unchanged (\datanum{k562_d_ip_noself_rc}, \datanum{rpe1_d_ip_noself_rc}). The curated regulatory tier adds nothing beyond STRING in this channel (\datanum{k562_d_ip_str_l1_rc} and \datanum{rpe1_d_ip_str_l1_rc} for L1+L3 relative to STRING alone): the signal comes from association evidence, i.e.\ from the response of functionally related genes, and the case with the largest gain (Section~\ref{sec:results_expl}) is supported by ribosomal-protein neighbours. Because the STRING combined score cannot be split by evidence channel here, we cannot exclude that part of it reflects text-mined knowledge of such screens.
Two simple heads on the same GRN do at least as well: a graph convolutional network (B5) and the mean training response of a gene's neighbours that are themselves training targets, which are better than the smoothing head by \datanum{k562_d_gcn_ip_rc} and \datanum{rpe1_d_gcn_ip_rc} (GCN) and by \datanum{k562_d_nbr_ip_rc} and \datanum{rpe1_d_nbr_ip_rc} (neighbour mean). The GCN loses its gain on the rewired graph (\datanum{k562_d_gcn_rew_rc} and \datanum{rpe1_d_gcn_rew_rc} in favour of the real GRN). We therefore regard the three heads as alternative read-outs of the same GRN; the smoothing head is the one fixed before evaluation. \datanumif{k562_gears_rc}{GEARS~\cite{roohani2024predicting}, trained on the same folds with its own gene-ontology graph (6 epochs and at most 100 cells per perturbation, below its defaults; \datanum{k562_gears_n} K562 and \datanum{rpe1_gears_n} RPE1 targets in its gene-ontology set; centred on its own mean prediction for training targets), reaches $\rho_c=$ \datanum{k562_gears_rc} (K562) and \datanum{rpe1_gears_rc} (RPE1); relative to it, the smoothing head differs by \datanum{k562_d_ip_gears_rc} and \datanum{rpe1_d_ip_gears_rc}.}
Propagating the observed responses of training targets over the GRN in several steps, as in network propagation~\cite{cowen2017network}, is the strongest read-out: random walks with restart on L1+L3 reach $\rho_c=$ \datanum{g12_k562_prop_L1pL3__rwr} (K562) and \datanum{g12_rpe1_prop_L1pL3__rwr} (RPE1), and propagating each tier separately and integrating them with non-negative weights fitted on validation targets reaches \datanum{g12_k562_prop_integrated__nnls} and \datanum{g12_rpe1_prop_integrated__nnls}, the best $\rho_c$ of all models in Table~\ref{tab:main} (Section~\ref{sec:ablation_tiers}).
Among the external models, biolord and CPA with gene-ontology descriptions of unseen targets (CPA at a reduced training budget) collapse towards the mean response ($\rho_c$ and centroid rank at chance), as does the zero-shot in silico knockout of scGPT. The released STATE checkpoints, whose pretraining data overlap with these screens and which are therefore not a held-out comparison, are the strongest external rows: the few-shot checkpoint exceeds the best iPerturb read-out in the centroid rank on K562 and in $\rho_c$ on RPE1, and is below it on the remaining metrics (Table~\ref{tab:main}).
The selected smoothing settings lie at the edge of the fixed grid (\datanum{k562_cfg}), so the reported gain may be a lower bound. The area under the precision--recall curve for differentially expressed genes is reported in Table~\ref{tab:main} but is prevalence-dominated: the no-change predictor attains the highest value in RPE1.

\subsection{The learnable Hill propagation is faithful to the exact Hill form and efficient as a matrix product}
\label{sec:results_hill}
\begin{figure}[t]
\centering
\includegraphics[width=\linewidth]{figures/fig2_operator.pdf}
\caption{\textbf{The regulatory operator.}
\textbf{(a)}~Modified Hill transfer functions of Eq.~\eqref{eq:hill} for five dissociation constants, the linear transfer, and the degree-8 Chebyshev form for $k=0.15$; the inset shows the maximal approximation error against the degree.
\textbf{(b)}~Messages of one regulator to three targets with a shared $k$ (rescaled copies of one shape) or edge-specific $k$ (three shapes).
\textbf{(c)}~A persistent clamp re-imposes the perturbed gene at every update, whereas a single injection lets it relax (four-gene motif with feedback; $x_2$ on the right axis).
\textbf{(d)}~Median speed-up of the SCC-aware solver over the global solver (numpy reference implementation).}
\label{fig:operator}
\end{figure}
\paragraph{Faithfulness.} The Chebyshev form approximates the Hill curve to \datanum{cheb_err_r32} at degree 32 and to \datanum{cheb_err_r16} at degree 16 (worst case over $k\in[0.05,1]$, Figure~\ref{fig:operator}a). Inside the full fixed-point iteration on the curated K562 graph with heterogeneous regulator constants and 200 clamped targets, the largest difference of the equilibrium from exact Hill propagation is \datanum{eq_cheb8_spmm_dx} at degree 8 and \datanum{eq_cheb16_spmm_dx} at degree 16 (decoded response up to \datanum{eq_cheb16_spmm_dd}, against a response scale of \datanum{eq_scale}); sparse and dense products agree exactly. The SCC-aware solver reproduces the global solver to \datanum{k562_scc_diff} (largest feedback component \datanum{k562_scc_largest} of \datanum{k562_scc_nodes} genes in K562, \datanum{rpe1_scc_largest} of \datanum{rpe1_scc_nodes} in RPE1) with a median speed-up of \datanum{k562_scc_speed}$\times$ (K562) and \datanum{rpe1_scc_speed}$\times$ (RPE1) in our reference implementation. Restricting the global solver to the genes reachable from the perturbed gene already gives \datanum{k562_scc_reach_only}$\times$ and \datanum{rpe1_scc_reach_only}$\times$; the component ordering adds a further \datanum{k562_scc_vs_reach}$\times$ and \datanum{rpe1_scc_vs_reach}$\times$.
\paragraph{Efficiency.} We separate two effects. First, aggregating messages as a matrix product instead of an edge-wise gather and scatter, which is independent of the transfer function, is slower on the sparse curated graph on an A100 (\datanum{bench_gpu_l1_128_E0_ms}\,ms vs.\ \datanum{bench_gpu_l1_128_E1_ms}\,ms per step at batch 128, 24 damped updates) but, on the 247k-edge association graph, reduces the step time from \datanum{bench_gpu_assoc_128_E0_ms}\,ms to \datanum{bench_gpu_assoc_128_E1_ms}\,ms and the peak memory from \datanum{bench_gpu_assoc_128_E0_mb}\,MB to \datanum{bench_gpu_assoc_128_E1_mb}\,MB at batch 128 (\datanum{bench_gpu_assoc_512_E0_ms}\,ms to \datanum{bench_gpu_assoc_512_E1_ms}\,ms and \datanum{bench_gpu_assoc_512_E0_mb}\,MB to \datanum{bench_gpu_assoc_512_E1_mb}\,MB at batch 512), i.e.\ \datanum{speedratio_lo}- to \datanum{speedratio_hi}-fold faster with \datanum{memratio_lo}- to \datanum{memratio_hi}-fold less memory (Figure~\ref{fig:ablation}c). Second, the Chebyshev expansion itself does not speed up a regulator-shared Hill curve with $n=2$, whose closed form is already cheap (\datanum{bench_gpu_assoc_128_C1_ms}\,ms at degree 16 and batch 128). Its value lies in edge-specific learnable shapes: edge-wise evaluation of per-edge Chebyshev shapes needs activations of size $B\times E\times r$ and runs out of memory on the 40\,GB device at batch 128 with $r=16$ and at batch 512 with $r=8$, whereas the matrix form stacks a basis of size $B\times G\times r$ and applies one sparse matrix with $Er$ non-zeros, fitting in \datanum{pe_spmm_r8_128_gb}\,GB and \datanum{pe_spmm_r16_128_gb}\,GB at batch 128 (\datanum{pe_memratio_r8_128}-fold less memory at $r=8$; Figure~\ref{fig:ablation}d; outputs agree to \datanum{pe_equiv}). On the CPU, the edge-wise form was the fastest or within a few per cent of the fastest in every shared-curve configuration measured (Appendix~\ref{app:bench}).
\paragraph{Predictive effect.} Whether the Hill form improves accuracy over a linear transfer is tested in Section~\ref{sec:ablation_hill}; it does not, so the operator is reported as a faithful and efficient implementation of the regulatory model rather than as a source of predictive gain.

\subsection{Predictions are explained by named neighbours and tested by ablation}
\label{sec:results_expl}
\begin{figure}[t]
\centering
\includegraphics[width=\linewidth]{figures/fig5_explain.pdf}
\caption{\textbf{Explanations of the association channel.}
\textbf{(a)}~Removing the top-$K$ attributed neighbours of a test target lowers its agreement with the observed profile more than removing $K$ random neighbours (mean difference with 95\% interval; targets with at least six neighbours).
\textbf{(b--d)}~Cases chosen by a rule fixed beforehand (K562 fold 0): largest gain over the linear baseline, smallest gain, and the first target without neighbours. Neighbours are association evidence, not causal paths.}
\label{fig:explain}
\end{figure}
For each test target we attribute the prediction to its association neighbours by the change in the predicted shift when a neighbour is removed (no observed data enter the attribution) and test the attribution by ablation against the observed profile. Removing the five top-attributed neighbours lowers the centred correlation more than removing five random neighbours by \datanum{k562_faith5} (K562, \datanum{k562_faith_n} targets) and \datanum{rpe1_faith5} (RPE1, \datanum{rpe1_faith_n}); for a single neighbour the difference is not distinguishable from zero (\datanum{k562_faith1}, \datanum{rpe1_faith1}). The attributions are therefore weakly but consistently faithful (Figure~\ref{fig:explain}a). The largest gain in the pre-specified K562 fold is DNTTIP2, whose three most attributed neighbours are the ribosomal proteins RPS4X, RPS18 and RPS6; the smallest gain is a target without neighbours, for which the shrunken embedding performs worse than the linear model, and the first neighbour-free target shows a small gain (Figure~\ref{fig:explain}b--d). In an exploratory analysis, falling back to the linear prediction for neighbour-free targets changes the centred correlation by at most \datanum{fallback_gain_max}, because such targets are only \datanum{k562_iso_pct}\% (K562) and \datanum{rpe1_iso_pct}\% (RPE1) of the test set.

\subsection{Explanations follow the regulatory mechanism at the first hop, not beyond}
\label{sec:results_mech}
\begin{figure}[t]
\centering
\includegraphics[width=\linewidth]{figures/fig_mechanism.pdf}
\caption{\textbf{Mechanism-level explanation and its test.}
\textbf{(a)}~A CRISPRi perturbation of A clamps A and lowers B, raises C (repressed by B) and lowers D, as computed by the model.
\textbf{(b)}~Sign accuracy of the predicted direction among responding genes ($|z|>3$) of held-out targets, by regulatory distance, for the real network and for controls with permuted edge signs or rewired edges (Wilson 95\% intervals, pooled over draws); the controls exceed 0.5 because most responding genes move in the same direction after a knockdown.
\textbf{(c)}~A mechanism report for one held-out K562 target: predicted sign (marker) against observed response (fill), supporting databases below.
\textbf{(d)}~Real-network accuracy at the first hop versus later hops, and the correlation between predicted and observed responses across reached genes.}
\label{fig:mechanism}
\end{figure}
We simulated CRISPRi as a persistent clamp on the signed curated network (CollecTRI, OmniPath and TRRUST, extended with signed SIGNOR transcriptional edges) and computed the steady state of Eq.~\eqref{eq:mech} with one fitted gain, then asked whether the explanation agrees with the measured response of held-out targets that have regulatory out-edges (\datanum{mech_k562_ntargets} in K562 and \datanum{mech_rpe1_ntargets} in RPE1 test perturbations). Among genes that respond (|z|$>3$), the predicted direction is correct for \datanum{mech_k562_real_h1}~\datanum{mech_k562_real_h1_ci} of the genes at one hop in K562 (\datanum{mech_k562_real_h1_n} genes) and for \datanum{mech_rpe1_real_h1}~\datanum{mech_rpe1_real_h1_ci} in RPE1 (\datanum{mech_rpe1_real_h1_n} genes), against \datanum{mech_k562_signperm_h1} and \datanum{mech_rpe1_signperm_h1} with permuted signs and \datanum{mech_k562_rewired_h1} and \datanum{mech_rpe1_rewired_h1} with rewired edges. The controls lie above chance because most responding genes shift in the same direction after a knockdown, so the relevant gain of the real network at the first hop is about \datanum{mech_k562_real_h1}~vs.~\datanum{mech_k562_signperm_h1} (K562) and \datanum{mech_rpe1_real_h1}~vs.~\datanum{mech_rpe1_signperm_h1} (RPE1). At two hops the accuracy is \datanum{mech_k562_real_h2} (K562) and \datanum{mech_rpe1_real_h2} (RPE1), at three hops \datanum{mech_k562_real_h3} and \datanum{mech_rpe1_real_h3}, and for four or more hops \datanum{mech_k562_real_h4p} and \datanum{mech_rpe1_real_h4p}; none of these exceeds the permuted-sign control, and the correlation between predicted and observed responses across all reached genes is \datanum{mech_k562_corr} and \datanum{mech_rpe1_corr}. The signed network therefore explains direct regulation, which is the biology the databases record, but the indirect signed cascade it predicts is not supported by these endpoint data: knockdown responses at distance are dominated by shared stress and growth programmes that the association channel, not the regulatory path, captures. We accordingly present paths beyond the first hop only as candidate mechanisms. The reported case, SSRP1 in K562, acts through MYC on ribosomal-protein genes (all edges activating, supported by CollecTRI and OmniPath), the predicted down-regulation matches three of the five most responsive genes shown, and the two with the opposite observed sign (RPLP1, UBA52) are the kind of compensatory response that a purely activating path cannot represent.
