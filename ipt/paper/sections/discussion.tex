\section{Discussion}
\label{sec:discussion}

We introduced iPerturb, which builds a cell-line-specific regulatory network from five evidence tiers and propagates perturbations through it with a learnable transfer function that follows the Hill function, so that every prediction can be traced to named genes and edges. On held-out CRISPRi targets in K562 and RPE1 the context-specific network carries predictive signal that matched rewired and data-derived graphs do not, and the Hill-form operator is an exact-to-$10^{-5}$, memory-lean implementation of the regulatory model on accelerators. Its one clear limitation is the one we set out to test: on the curated signed core, which reaches only 5--7\% of the perturbed genes in these essential-gene screens, a nonlinear Hill transfer does not predict better than a linear one, and the gain over a linear low-rank model comes from association evidence rather than from curated regulation. We read this as a statement about coverage, not about biology: the screens perturb mostly non-TF genes, and a signed core cannot propagate what it does not reach.

Several limitations qualify these results. The predicted endpoint is the mean response, not a distribution of cells. RNA abundance is a proxy for regulator activity and endpoint data cannot identify time constants, so the fixed-point operator is a steady-state model; neighbour attributions are tested by ablation but are association paths, not validated mechanisms. The folds and seeds reuse the same biological screens, and graph and no-graph models were tuned on comparable but not identical budgets; matched controls share their grid. The largest gains are obtained for genes in well-characterised complexes (for example, ribosomal-protein neighbours), which are enriched in essential-gene screens, and the transfer to screens with few such complexes is untested. The GeneHancer tier is empty for RPE1, so the cell-line-specific tier is tested in K562 only. Future work will add more cell lines and a genome-wide screen with more transcription-factor targets, and test edge-specific learnable shapes, which the matrix-multiplication form now makes affordable, on data in which a signed core has more reach.
