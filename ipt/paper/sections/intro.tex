\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:introduction}

Genome-scale CRISPR screens with single-cell read-out now measure the transcriptional response to thousands of genetic perturbations in a single experiment~\cite{replogle2022mapping}, yet every screen covers only a fraction of the genes, combinations and cell types of interest.
Predicting the response to a perturbation that was never measured would let such screens be prioritised and extended \emph{in silico}, and it is the task addressed by graph-based predictors such as GEARS~\cite{roohani2024predicting}.
A useful predictor must therefore generalise to unseen target genes, be specific to the cellular context, and explain why a gene is predicted to change~\cite{bunne2024build}.

The first obstacle lies in the network a predictor propagates over.
GEARS propagates over a Gene Ontology graph and a co-expression graph~\cite{roohani2024predicting}, which encode functional association rather than direct regulation, and Genetic Neural Networks follow transcription-factor (TF) regulation but order their layers after removing feedback edges~\cite{eetemadi2019genetic}.
Curated collections such as CollecTRI provide signed TF--target interactions~\cite{mullerdott2023collectri}, but they are generic rather than cell-line-specific, cover TFs unevenly, and mix interactions with very different levels of evidence.
A GRN for perturbation modelling has to combine heterogeneous evidence, keep the cell line in view, and record which edges are regulatory and which are merely associative.

The second obstacle lies in how a perturbation is propagated.
Linear propagation, such as random walks over a network, is cheap and convergent but cannot represent saturation or the composition of activating and repressing inputs.
Graph neural networks over a regulatory graph, as in SPECTRA~\cite{calabro2026spectra}, are flexible but learn their message functions rather than imposing a regulatory transfer law, and logic-based ODE models with Hill-type transfer functions give each edge its own parameters~\cite{eduati2017}, which scales poorly to genome-wide networks.
Because simple linear baselines remain hard to beat on unseen perturbations~\cite{ahlmanneltze2025comparison}, nonlinearity has to be introduced with few parameters, a guaranteed steady state and an evaluation that controls for the network itself.

In this paper, we introduce iPerturb, a biologically informed and explainable framework that addresses both obstacles in two linked phases (Figure~\ref{fig:overview}).
First, iPerturb assembles a context-specific GRN for each cell line from five evidence tiers, from curated signed regulation to responses of training perturbations.
Second, it propagates an unseen perturbation through this GRN: the perturbed gene is clamped, regulatory edges pass their input through a learnable Hill transfer function shared per regulator, and association edges carry observed training responses over multiple hops.
The GRN of the first phase fixes \emph{where} a perturbation can travel; the propagation of the second phase fixes \emph{how} it travels and which edges explain each predicted change.

Our contributions are twofold.
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Context-specific GRN construction.} We build a five-tier GRN per cell line that separates signed, directed regulation from undirected association evidence, derives all response-based edges from training perturbations only, and combines tier-specific multi-hop propagation with weights fitted for each cell line.
On \datanum{n_uniq_total} held-out CRISPRi targets in K562 and RPE1, this raises the centred correlation of the predicted response from \datanum{k562_b2_rc} to \datanum{g12_k562_prop_integrated__nnls} (K562) and from \datanum{rpe1_b2_rc} to \datanum{g12_rpe1_prop_integrated__nnls} (RPE1) over a linear baseline, whereas a degree-preserving rewired network with the same read-out loses the gain.
\item \textbf{Learnable Hill-based regulatory propagation.} We propagate perturbations with regulator-shared Hill transfer functions under a persistent clamp, prove that the clamped steady state exists and is unique, and evaluate the operator as a matrix product in a Chebyshev basis.
It reproduces exact Hill propagation to \datanum{eq_cheb16_spmm_dx}, uses \datanum{memratio_lo}- to \datanum{memratio_hi}-fold less memory on a dense graph, and its signed paths predict the direction of first-hop responses in \datanum{mech_k562_real_h1_pct}\% (K562) and \datanum{mech_rpe1_real_h1_pct}\% (RPE1) of responding genes.
\end{itemize}
Here, \emph{explainable} is meant mechanistically: each predicted change decomposes into contributions of named neighbours and signed paths of the GRN, each supported by named databases; paths are candidate mechanisms, not validated molecular routes.
