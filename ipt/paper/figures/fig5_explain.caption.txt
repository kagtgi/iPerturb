Fig. 5. Association neighbours that the smoothing model attributes most to its prediction are, on average, the ones whose removal hurts agreement with the observed profile most; the gain is not uniform across targets. (a) Faithfulness of neighbour attributions for the smooth L1+L3 model (K562 essential, n=651 targets; RPE1, n=689 targets): drop in centered Pearson when the top-K attributed association neighbours are removed minus the drop when K random neighbours are removed (random drop averaged over 50 draws; values averaged over folds per target; points are means with 95% paired-bootstrap CIs, 10,000 resamples). The zero line marks no difference. The CI excludes zero only at K=5 in both cell lines (K562: K=5 [0.0011, 0.0158]; RPE1: K=5 [0.0053, 0.0410]); the effects are small. Attribution of neighbour j is a_j = <pred(N) - pred(N\j), pred(N) - train mean>. (b-d) Three K562 fold-0 case studies selected by a predefined rule, not by inspection: gain = centered Pearson of smooth L1+L3 minus that of the linear baseline B2 per test target; (b) maximum gain (DNTTIP2, 102 association neighbours; three largest attributions: RPS4X, RPS18, RPS6), (c) minimum gain (VARS, 0 association neighbours), (d) alphabetically first test target with zero association neighbours (GARS). Scatter: observed versus predicted delta for the 40 genes with largest absolute observed delta, perturbed gene excluded; B2 in light red, smooth L1+L3 in blue; r is the Pearson correlation over these 40 genes (b: B2 0.57, L1+L3 0.93; c: B2 0.91, L1+L3 0.74; d: B2 0.63, L1+L3 0.69). The association graph is the union of curated L1 edges and STRING pairs, treated as undirected; neighbours are statistical associations, not causal paths.
