Fig. 2. The iPT operator. (a) Transfer functions on [0,1]: identity (grey dashed) and the exact Hill transfer H(x;k) with n=2 for k = 0.05, 0.15, 0.3, 0.6, 1 (blue ramp, light to dark); the red dashed curve is the degree-8 Chebyshev approximation for k = 0.15. Right: maximum absolute difference between exact Hill and Chebyshev over x in [0,1] and 25 log-spaced k in [0.05,1], for degrees r = 4, 8, 16, 32 (log scale; 1.3e-1, 6.0e-2, 2.7e-3, 1.9e-4). (b) One regulator with three targets (signed weights +0.8, -0.5, +0.3), message w[H(x)-H(x0)] at x0 = 0.3. With a regulator-shared k = 0.3 the three messages are rescalings of one shape (1 k + 3 w = 4 parameters); with per-edge k = 0.1, 0.3, 0.9 the shapes differ (3 k + 3 w = 6 parameters). (c) Four-gene motif 0->1 (+), 1->2 (+), 2->3 (+), 3->1 (-) (weights +-0.9), damped fixed-point iteration from a baseline of 0.5 with H of k = 0.3, n = 2, and gene 0 knocked down by a fraction 0.8 (target value 0.1). Solid: persistent clamp, the mode used by the model (target re-imposed at every iteration); dashed: single injection (target released after step 0). Gene 0 uses the left axis and gene 2 the right axis (different scales). Under the persistent clamp, gene 0 stays at 0.1 and gene 2 settles at about 0.493; under single injection both genes return to 0.5. (d) Median speed-up of the SCC-aware solver over the global solver for 12 targets in each of two cell-line graphs (K562 essential: 8563 nodes, largest SCC 297; RPE1: 8749 nodes, largest SCC 321), with the largest SCC size and the maximum absolute difference between the two solutions annotated (1.2e-13 and 1.1e-13). Speed-up is measured in a numpy/python reference implementation, not an optimized kernel.
