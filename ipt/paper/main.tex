\begin{abstract}
Building an interpretable model that predicts genome-wide transcriptional responses to genetic perturbations remains a grand challenge in biology. Mechanistic kinetic models are difficult to scale to the genome, whereas deep neural network--based models often lack interpretability. To bridge this gap, we propose iPerturb, a biologically informed and explainable framework for context-specific single-cell perturbation response modelling. iPerturb first integrates five tiers of regulatory and association evidence into a cell-line-specific gene regulatory network (GRN); then propagates a perturbation through it with a learnable transfer function that follows the Hill function of gene regulation, aggregated as a matrix product; and finally explains each prediction through the named neighbours that support it. Evaluated on held-out CRISPRi targets in two human cell lines (\datanum{k562_nuniq} in K562 and \datanum{rpe1_nuniq} in RPE1), the tiered GRN raises the centred correlation of the predicted response over a linear baseline by \datanum{k562_d_ip_b2_rc} and \datanum{rpe1_d_ip_b2_rc} (95\% intervals), whereas degree-preserving rewired and data-derived graphs give none. The matrix-product form lowers peak memory \datanum{memratio_lo}- to \datanum{memratio_hi}-fold on a dense graph, and a Chebyshev form of the Hill curve, which reproduces exact Hill propagation to \datanum{eq_cheb16_spmm_dx}, makes edge-specific learnable shapes feasible. On the curated signed core, which reaches \datanum{k562_signed_out_pct}--\datanum{rpe1_signed_out_pct}\% of the perturbed genes, Hill-form propagation does not predict better than linear propagation.
\keywords{gene expression prediction \and genetic perturbation \and gene regulatory network \and biologically informed neural network \and Hill function \and interpretable AI}
\end{abstract}

\caption{\textbf{Overview of the iPerturb framework.} (1)~A cell line and a CRISPRi target are the inputs; the response is the perturbation-conditioned mean expression change. (2)~A context-specific GRN is assembled from five evidence tiers (L1 curated signed regulation; L2 enhancers and binding sites active in the cell line; L3 protein association; L4 co-expression; L5 training-perturbation response), with association evidence kept undirected and unsigned. (3)~The perturbation is propagated by a learnable transfer function of Hill form, evaluated in a Chebyshev basis as a matrix product, with the perturbed gene clamped. (4)~The predicted expression change is (5)~explained by supporting neighbours and edges, tested by ablation. Graphs and bars are illustrative.}
